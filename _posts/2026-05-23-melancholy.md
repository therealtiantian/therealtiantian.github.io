Melancholy is a difficult thing. Rage is easy, you just go to sleep, or take a walk; rage also happens only rarely to an average person. Passion includes strength, and you can usually channel that to something good. Melancholy is the worst feeling, because it passively drains one’s motivation for anything, even for menlancholy itself. Melancholy needs no force; it is about drifting along, and the melancholy person does not even have the power to stop it, or does not want to. Free will, in this case, is almost laughable. 

I once wanted to learn how to wash dishes properly. There is a gentleman on YouTube, wearing white shirt, a tie, and suit pants, elegantly showing the process. When he grabbed the sponge, he mentions that if the water is not totally squeezed, there will be a sour smell. “Sour is a difficult smell,” he mentions. I enjoyed this line much more than anything else he have said in the video, and whenever I find something that is hard to qualify, I think of the sour smell. Melancholy is a difficult disposition. That who indulges in melancholy is miserable, because it is also embarassing to talk about melancholy. It sounds like whinning, and whinning turns a conversation into tasteless noise. 

Melancholy is also narcissistic. When one sees other people suffer, the feeling is called sympathy. That is not really about melancholy, but more anger and sadness. Melancholy means someone mopes over their own existence, and finds no possible manner of alleviation. Its utter pettiness turns the person into a mirror-looking, pathetic being. 

What is good is that, just like all emotions, melancholy goes away, too, though it might feel longer, because there is no drama included, and a better condition might feel less of a relief. But there is something exceptional about melancholy, if I may use myself to illustrate. I have a bad habit of obsessively telling jokes for everything possible, and it annoys myself at times, and might be true for others around me as well, who tolerate me with great generosity. But when I am melancholy, I drop that obsession. I am content with being a bland person, simply existing, not as a chronic entertainer. It is healthy to be content with one’s natural temperament. And when I am melancholy, I am, ironically, temporarily someone I strive to be.
