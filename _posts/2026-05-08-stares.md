I’ve gradually developed the involuntary habit of staring at people, and finally I understand why there are “Swiss stares”! It’s not out of nowhere, actually, it comes naturally out of the social interaction system.

In the Chinese society, people are obliged to make interactions when eyes meet, even if they do not know each other at all. (Staring is a social cue! 你瞅啥？) But in Switzerland, the norm is that only acquaintances have a small interaction, and only friends have a real interaction. That makes staring very comfy, because it (almost) never invites any unwanted interaction. I often stare at a random person, either because they stand out for their beauty, or because they are so ordinary that I do not even need to use any cognitive effort, or because they sit or stand at a point my eyes are comfortable to take a rest on. If people stare at me, I can also understand, because I am such an easy point to put the eyes on when they are not making a conscious effort, since I look different. Some people deliberately not to look at me because their cognitive system is evoked, and they know it is impolite to look at a foreigner, since I might misinterprete. 

It is very liberating to be able to stare. Of course, just like in any other societies, one would not want to stare at an acquaintance, especially who is in the middle between stranger and friend, then it is obliged to make an awkward conversation. Even the Swiss avoid to stare at such acquaintances. Of course, me too. I usually stare at someone who I definitely do not know. But if I am not sure if I know them, I would stare away to err on the safer side. 

Sometimes, staring is taboo, like people with prosthesis, etc. But I don’t really care, because I think that is really, really cool, so I look at them sometimes as an involuntary appreciation. But I do not stare at them of course, because that would be too conscious, too effort taking.
